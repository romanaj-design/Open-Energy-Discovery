# Open Energy Discovery

**Building AI course project**

## Summary

Open Energy Discovery is a proposed AI-assisted platform where people can share unusual observations, experimental results and scientific ideas that might lead to new energy technologies. AI would help connect related findings, identify promising research directions and bring relevant evidence to the attention of scientists.

## Background

Many people encounter interesting natural phenomena, unexpected material properties or unusual results during their work, hobbies or scientific experiments. Some observations may be scientifically valuable, but they can remain unnoticed because they are not shared, documented or connected to existing research.

Scientific knowledge is distributed across many disciplines, publications and individual researchers. A potentially useful connection between two findings may be difficult to discover when they come from different fields.

The motivation behind this project is to explore whether artificial intelligence and collective human observation could help identify research opportunities that might otherwise be overlooked.

The long-term vision is to contribute to the discovery of new ways of generating, collecting or storing energy. The project does not assume that a completely new energy source necessarily exists or that AI can discover one on its own. Instead, it aims to improve how potentially relevant observations are collected, evaluated and investigated.

## How is it used?

The proposed platform would allow people from different backgrounds to submit observations and findings.

A submission could include:
- A description of the observed phenomenon.
- Photographs, measurements, experimental results or other supporting evidence.
- The conditions under which the observation was made.
- The submitter's interpretation or research question, clearly distinguished from measured facts.

AI would help organize submissions, identify similar observations, search relevant scientific literature and flag missing information or possible explanations.

Rather than automatically rejecting unusual claims, the system would assign preliminary categories based on evidence quality, relevance and uncertainty.

Researchers could browse the findings, investigate promising connections and contact contributors when further information is needed. Qualified experts would make the final decisions about scientific validity and whether further experiments are justified.

Potential users include researchers, engineers, technicians, students, amateur scientists and people who encounter unusual phenomena in nature or everyday life.

## Data sources and AI methods

Potential data sources include:
- Observations and experimental results submitted by users.
- Open-access scientific publications and research repositories.
- Publicly available datasets relating to materials, physical phenomena and energy technologies.
- Verified measurements and results from documented experiments.

Possible AI techniques include:

- **Natural language processing and semantic search:** to understand descriptions and retrieve related research, even when different terminology is used.
- **Clustering and similarity detection:** to group related observations and identify connections between submissions.
- **Anomaly detection:** to flag unusual measurements or patterns for further investigation.
- **Information extraction and classification:** to organize submissions and identify missing experimental details.
- **Human-in-the-loop evaluation:** to combine AI-assisted screening with expert review.

The first prototype could focus on a small collection of documented observations and scientific publications. It could demonstrate how AI-assisted search and clustering help identify relationships between findings.

Any potential energy-related claim would require independent scientific verification. The system would not treat a lack of matching publications as proof that a discovery is new.

## Challenges

The main challenges include:

- Distinguishing meaningful observations from measurement errors, misunderstandings and unsupported claims.
- Avoiding the loss of unusual but potentially valuable ideas during automated screening.
- Ensuring that AI-generated summaries and recommendations are accurate and traceable to their sources.
- Protecting personal information and respecting intellectual property rights.
- Establishing clear rules for data quality, scientific review and responsible experimentation.
- Avoiding misleading claims about perpetual motion, energy creation or technologies that violate established physical principles.

The platform could help identify research opportunities, but it could not guarantee a discovery, replace scientific experiments or establish that a proposed energy technology is physically possible.

## What next?

The project could develop in several stages:

1. Create a structured database of observations and relevant scientific publications.
2. Build a simple prototype that accepts submissions and searches for related findings.
3. Test AI-assisted classification and similarity detection on a small, documented dataset.
4. Invite researchers to evaluate the usefulness and reliability of the suggested connections.
5. Expand the platform to additional scientific fields and establish partnerships with research institutions.

A future version could also help contributors formulate testable hypotheses and identify the measurements or experiments needed to investigate them.

Further development would require programming, access to reliable scientific data, feedback from researchers and expertise in relevant scientific disciplines.
